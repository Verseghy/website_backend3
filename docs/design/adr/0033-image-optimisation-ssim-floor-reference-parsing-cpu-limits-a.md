# 0033. Image optimisation: SSIM floor, reference parsing, CPU limits and PNG chunks

- Status: Accepted (2026-10-26)
- Decision questions: none: records the maintainer's answers of 2026-10-25 to the decision-register items D73, D77, D78, D79, D82 and D86 (research: DECISIONS R15), which settle what the three image-optimisation slices found where [ADR 0015](0015-media-separate-static-server-media-library-image-optimisatio.md) §7 is silent or narrower than what they built, and the answers of 2026-10-26 to the third register's items D100 to D104 (research: DECISIONS R16), which settle what implementing those six answers left open, and D111 (research: DECISIONS R20), which settles what implementing the answer to D103 left open. It amends ADR 0015 §7, which records Q27 (the image half), and the "Image tests" of its "What must be true in code and tests".

## Context

[ADR 0015](0015-media-separate-static-server-media-library-image-optimisatio.md) §7 optimises every new image upload before its URL exists. These are the parts this ADR touches:

| Rule | What ADR 0015 §7 says |
|---|---|
| JPEG row | "First the metadata is stripped losslessly. Then the image is re-encoded at quality 85, the visually lossless target. The re-encoded bytes are kept only if they are at least 5 % smaller than the stripped original. Otherwise the stripped original is kept." |
| PNG row | "Lossless recompression only. Ancillary chunks are dropped, except the colour chunks (`iCCP`, `sRGB`, `gAMA`, `cHRM`) and an `eXIf` chunk reduced to its Orientation tag." |
| WebP row | "Metadata is removed. Lossy WebP is re-encoded at the same quality target and kept only under the same at-least-5 % rule as JPEG. Lossless WebP keeps its pixels and animation." |
| Guards | "An image that does not decode as its sniffed type is rejected as corrupt. An image above a pixel budget (configurable, default 50 megapixels) is rejected before it is decoded." |
| Where it runs | "Inside the upload request, on a blocking thread. A semaphore allows one optimisation at a time per backend3 process." |
| Encoder | "The media slice picks the library by measurement: output size, perceptual similarity, CPU time on the node, and build cost on the hub." |
| Image tests | "every re-encoded JPEG and lossy WebP in the corpus reaches SSIM ≥ 0.98 on the luma channel at full resolution, compared with the stripped original" |

The three slices that implement §7, image-optimise-jpeg, image-optimise-png and image-optimise-webp-gif, found six places where §7 does not hold as written or says nothing (research: decision register D73, D77, D78, D79, D82, D86):

1. **Quality 85 against SSIM 0.98 (D73).** MozJPEG at quality 85 left 10 of 20 measured CC0 photographs below a luma SSIM of 0.98 (mean 0.978, lowest 0.950); quality 90 left 3 of 20, at 0.257 and 0.790 of the stripped bytes for the camera and phone sets instead of 0.199 and 0.590. The photographs below 0.98 have fine texture: foliage, fur, water, a night scene. The corpus's quality-95 camera original reached 0.974, so the corpus test had to list it as below the ADR's own target ([image-encoders.md](../image-encoders.md)). Lossy WebP's lowest result was 0.9839, but only on ImageMagick's built-in pictures and blurred fractals, never on textured photographs. The only SSIM implementation was test code that kept five sums per pixel, 40 bytes per pixel, about 2 GB at 50 megapixels.
2. **Chroma and colour models (D77).** The JPEG re-encode writes MozJPEG's 4:2:0 chroma even for a 4:4:4 original, which a luma SSIM cannot see; a corpus test bounds the mean chroma difference at 8 levels, and the measured maximum was 4.34. RGB, CMYK and YCCK JPEGs, rare and mostly from print workflows, are decoded as the check and keep their stripped original, which narrows "the image is re-encoded at quality 85".
3. **Strict parsing (D78).** image-optimise-webp-gif refused some shapes, part of which libwebp and the `gif` crate decode (a GIF without its trailer, a graphic control extension that is not 4 bytes, an `ANIM` chunk under 6 bytes, image chunks or nested frames after a frame's image inside an `ANMF`; that slice adds the list to [image-encoders.md](../image-encoders.md)) and dropped others (metadata chunks after a frame's image, which libwebp reads as file-level chunks; GIF plain-text extensions; reserved VP8X flags). It strips animated lossy WebP and never re-encodes it. image-optimise-jpeg's EXIF reader dropped the orientation of three malformed shapes that some browsers read: IFD0 without its next-IFD offset, a first Orientation tag that is not a SHORT of count 1, and a second EXIF segment. The merged precedent, image-sniff-budget (Verseghy/website_backend3#211), followed the reference decoders, "so no reader refuses anything its reference decoder accepts", because refusing more would be stricter than §7's definition of corrupt.
4. **CPU limits (D79).** §7 bounds an upload only by its per-canvas pixel budget. image-optimise-webp-gif refuses a WebP animation of more than 4096 frames or more than 256 Mi pixels summed over its frames, webpx's defaults for untrusted input, before decoding. A GIF has no such limit: its check costs about 0.75 s per MB measured, up to about 180 s for a crafted 128 MiB file at the LZW bound, past the 120 s `/api/admin` timeout, all the while holding the one-at-a-time semaphore. image-optimise-jpeg found no cap on the number of progressive scans, a known CPU-exhaustion vector.
5. **PNG time at the budget (D82).** oxipng at preset 2 took 57.7 CPU seconds for a PNG at the pixel budget, and a 50-megapixel camera-sized PNG took about 32 s at preset 1 and 122.5 s at preset 3, against the 120 s `/api/admin` timeout of [ADR 0023](0023-operations-hardening-rate-limits-monitoring-backups-secrets.md) §5 and §7. The edge node's cores are slower or unknown.
6. **PNG chunks (D86).** The PNG row's list drops the APNG animation chunks (`acTL`, `fcTL`, `fdAT`), so an APNG is stored as its first frame, and the PNG third-edition HDR chunks (`cICP`, `mDCV`, `cLLI`), so an HDR PNG may display differently. The same §7 keeps animation for WebP and GIF.

The maintainer answered all six on 2026-10-25 (research: DECISIONS R15), each with the recommended option. Each item below gives the register's question, the answer, and the consequence the register gave for that option (research: decision register D73, D77, D78, D79, D82, D86):

## Decision

### 1. The keep rule of a lossy re-encode (D73)

- **The rule.** A JPEG is still re-encoded at quality 85, and a still lossy WebP at the same target. The re-encode is kept only when both hold:
  1. it is at least 5 % smaller than the stripped original, as before;
  2. its mean SSIM on the luma channel at full resolution, compared with the decoded stripped original, is at least **0.98**.

  Otherwise the stripped original is stored. This replaces "kept only if they are at least 5 % smaller" in §7's JPEG row and "kept only under the same at-least-5 % rule as JPEG" in its WebP row.
- **The measure** is the one §7's image tests name: the SSIM of Wang, Bovik, Sheikh and Simoncelli (2004), with an 11 × 11 Gaussian window of σ = 1.5, K1 = 0.01, K2 = 0.03 and L = 255, averaged over every position where the window fits, with no downsampling. An image narrower or shorter than the 11-pixel window cannot be measured and keeps its stripped original.
- **The order.** The size test runs first, because it is free; only a re-encode that passes it is decoded again, to its luma, for the SSIM.
- **One implementation.** The SSIM lives in `src/domain/image_optimise/ssim.rs` (`LumaSsim`, `mean_ssim`), pure under [ADR 0008](0008-code-layout-a-pure-src-domain-beside-loco-models.md), and the keep rule in `src/domain/image_optimise/keep.rs` (`reencode_is_kept`, `reencode_meets_ssim_floor`, `SSIM_FLOOR`). The corpus tests call the same functions, so what they check is what an upload is held to. It gives bit for bit the values of the test code that the encoder measurement used.
- **Cost.** `LumaSsim` takes the two images one row at a time and keeps only the 11 rows its window needs, about 520 bytes per image column (4.3 MiB at the 8,660 columns of a 50-megapixel photograph). It runs inside the blocking optimisation of §7 ("Where it runs"), after the encoder has freed its buffers. Measured at the budget: 1.3 to 1.8 CPU seconds, and about 147 MiB above what the optimiser already holds, most of it libjpeg's coefficient buffer for the progressive re-encode; the encoder before it needs more, so the peak of a JPEG optimisation does not rise ([image-encoders.md](../image-encoders.md)). For a still lossy WebP, image-optimise-webp-gif measured the pass as the new peak of the opaque path, 214 MB at 49 megapixels instead of the encoder's 182 MB, below the transparent path's 254 MB; that slice adds the measurement to the same file.
- **Implemented by:** image-optimise-jpeg for JPEG (this ADR's PR); image-optimise-webp-gif for still lossy WebP, calling the same module.

### 2. JPEG chroma and colour models (D77)

- The JPEG re-encode writes 4:2:0 chroma, MozJPEG's default, for every YCbCr image, a 4:4:4 original included. The corpus test that bounds the mean chroma difference of every re-encode at 8 levels stays, and the corpus keeps a 4:4:4 original that is re-encoded.
- Greyscale and YCbCr JPEGs are re-encoded. RGB, CMYK and YCCK JPEGs are decoded as the check and keep their stripped original; they are not re-encoded. This replaces "Then the image is re-encoded at quality 85" in §7's JPEG row for those three colour models.
- **Implemented by:** image-optimise-jpeg (this ADR's PR).

### 3. Parsing that follows the reference decoders (D78)

Where §7 is silent, each reader accepts what the reference decoder of its format accepts, and refuses only what that decoder refuses, as image-sniff-budget (#211) does, with the one exception of a GIF with no image (below). §7's definition of corrupt, "an image that does not decode as its sniffed type", is unchanged.

- **WebP and GIF.** The references are libwebp and the `gif` crate. Each shape that image-optimise-webp-gif refuses or drops (Context, item 3, the missing GIF trailer included) is checked against them; what they decode passes, and what they read, such as a metadata chunk after a frame's image that libwebp reads as a file-level chunk, is read the same way. image-optimise-webp-gif adds to [image-encoders.md](../image-encoders.md) the list of every shape it checked, with what libwebp's decoder and its demuxer, or the `gif` crate, do with it and what the strip does. **Implemented by:** image-optimise-webp-gif.
- **One exception: a GIF with no image (D104).** A GIF that holds no image descriptor, only its logical screen and perhaps extensions, is refused as corrupt, with or without its trailer, although the `gif` crate decodes one in which an extension comes before the trailer, with no frame: no viewer shows a picture of such a file, and libwebp refuses a WebP with no frame in the same way. **Implemented by:** image-optimise-webp-gif.
- **Animated lossy WebP** has its metadata removed, and its frames are kept as uploaded: it is not re-encoded. This replaces "Lossy WebP is re-encoded" in §7's WebP row for animations; a still lossy WebP is re-encoded under section 1. **Implemented by:** image-optimise-webp-gif.
- **The EXIF Orientation tag** is read as Chromium, the engine of most of the site's visitors and editors, reads it, with Skia's EXIF parser (`skia@62095415:rust/exif/exif_parse.rs`, which Chromium builds in through `SK_EXIF_PARSE_WITH_RUST`, `chromium/src@bd57e07c:skia/BUILD.gn`):
  - the entries of IFD0 are read as long as each is whole, so a missing next-IFD offset (the first shape of D78) or a count that claims more entries than the data holds does not drop the orientation;
  - the first Orientation entry that is a SHORT of count 1 with a value from 1 to 8 counts, and an earlier one of another type, count or value is skipped (the second shape);
  - the first ExifIFD pointer of IFD0 (tag `0x8769`, LONG, count 1) is followed at its place in IFD0's order, so an Orientation in the EXIF sub-IFD counts when none came before it; a pointer of another type or count is skipped, and a later well-formed one is followed;
  - in a JPEG, only the first `APP1` segment that starts with `Exif\0` and holds more than its 6-byte identifier is read, whatever the identifier's padding byte (`skia@62095415:src/codec/SkJpegMetadataDecoderImpl.cpp`). An orientation in a second EXIF segment (the third shape) is therefore dropped with that segment, as Chromium ignores it too.

  Three of these rules come from following Skia, not from the register's list of three shapes: the EXIF sub-IFD, an `APP1` segment that holds only the `Exif` identifier not counting as the first EXIF segment, and the unchecked padding byte. The maintainer kept all of them, with the first two shapes, on 2026-10-26 (D100, "Keep the Chromium reading as built"; Context).

  ADR 0015 §7 never lets the output grow, and the reference reading keeps that. Every structure the reader accepts holds IFD0 at offset 8 or later with at least one whole entry, so it is at least 22 bytes long. The replacement is the 26-byte TIFF structure of the Orientation tag alone when the original is at least that long, and otherwise its first 22 bytes, without the next-IFD offset: the shape of such an original, which Chromium and Firefox read. The one shape Chromium reads and backend3 does not is IFD0 starting inside the 8-byte TIFF header, which no writer produces and which can be shorter than 22 bytes; its orientation is dropped.

  Firefox (`mozilla-central@08f5d929b54d:image/decoders/EXIF.cpp`) also reads IFD0 without its next-IFD offset and also follows the ExifIFD pointer, so for the first shape and the sub-IFD it turns the upload and the stored file alike. It differs from Chromium in two cases, and the stored file, which holds only Chromium's value, changes how Firefox shows them: it stops at an Orientation entry that is not a SHORT of count 1 with a value from 1 to 8, so it showed an upload of the second shape as stored and turns the stored file as Chromium does; and of two valid Orientation entries it takes the last where Chromium takes the first.

  **Implemented by:** image-optimise-jpeg for the JPEG `APP1` segment (this ADR's PR). The PNG `eXIf` chunk and the WebP `EXIF` chunk hold the same TIFF structure and are read by the same rule: image-optimise-png and image-optimise-webp-gif use this reader (`src/domain/image_optimise/exif.rs`), which lands with this ADR.

### 4. CPU limits for crafted uploads (D79)

§7's guards gain four refusals, each checked from the structure of the file before anything is decoded, and each with its own message rather than the corrupt-image one, since nothing says such a file is corrupt:

| Format | Limit | Implemented by |
|---|---|---|
| WebP animation | At most 4096 frames, and at most 256 Mi (268,435,456) pixels summed over all frames, each frame at its own size: webpx's defaults for untrusted input, as built. That is five frames at the default budget, or about 870 frames of 640 × 480. | image-optimise-webp-gif |
| GIF | The same two totals, over the frames that the GIF's block structure declares. | image-optimise-webp-gif |
| APNG | The same two totals (D102), over the default image and the frames that the animation's chunks alone keep under section 6, each at its own size: the default image's pixels always count, and it counts as a frame only when it is frame 0. They are counted from `IHDR` and the `fcTL` chunks before any image data is inflated, the default image's included, so a frame whose data then turns out not to decode still counts. | image-optimise-png |
| JPEG | At most **100** scans (SOS segments), counted by the metadata strip before the decode check. The editor is asked to save the image again; every common encoder writes a handful of scans. | image-optimise-jpeg (this ADR's PR) |

The JPEG cap's value is set by measurement, as the answer's consequence asks, and the maintainer kept the measured value on 2026-10-26 (D101, "Keep 100, as built"; Context). libjpeg passes over every block of the image for each scan, whatever data the scan holds, so a scan's cost follows the pixel count, not its bytes. The costliest scans measured at the 50-megapixel budget, AC refinement scans of end-of-band runs, take about 27 ms of decoding each ([image-encoders.md](../image-encoders.md)):

- at 100 scans they add at most about 2.8 CPU seconds to an upload;
- libjpeg-turbo, the decoder of MozJPEG, Chromium and Firefox, refuses more than 500 scans when a TurboJPEG application asks it to refuse "an unreasonably large number of scans" (`TJFLAG_LIMITSCANS`, `libjpeg-turbo@36956793:src/turbojpeg.c`), the guard its ChangeLog introduced after the report "Two Issues with the JPEG Standard". 500 would allow about 14 CPU seconds on the measuring machine, more on the node's slower cores, while a second upload waits behind the one-at-a-time semaphore within the same 120 s `/api/admin` timeout;
- 100 is libjpeg-turbo's own limit for the scan scripts that its cjpeg and jpegtran read (`MAX_SCANS`, `src/rdswitch.c`), so no progressive file those tools can write is refused. Real files are far below it: libjpeg's progressive script writes 10 scans for a colour image, MozJPEG wrote 4 to 11 for every re-encode measured, and a sequential JPEG has one scan per component.

Without a cap, a 128 MiB upload could hold hundreds of thousands of such scans, hours of decoding with the semaphore held.

media-upload-core maps the four refusals to Hungarian messages, as it does every other refusal of §7 ([ADR 0021](0021-hungarian-only-admin-ui.md) §4).

### 5. PNG optimisation preset by image size (D82)

- PNG keeps lossless recompression with oxipng. Up to a pixel-count threshold it uses preset 2, the preset its measurement chose; above it, a cheaper preset (1 or lower), so that a PNG at the pixel budget finishes inside the 120 s `/api/admin` timeout with room for the upload's transfer and a second upload queued behind the semaphore. This stays within §7's "Encoder: the media slice picks the library by measurement", with the presets and the threshold measured.
- The threshold is a setting, validated at startup and passed to the optimiser as an argument ([ADR 0008](0008-code-layout-a-pure-src-domain-beside-loco-models.md)). Its production value is set at deploy from a measurement on the production node, whose cores are not those of the machine the slices measured on.
- **Implemented by:** image-optimise-png (the two presets and the setting); k8s-backend3-deploy measures on the node and sets the production value.

### 6. PNG chunks: APNG animation and HDR colour (D86, D103, D111)

§7's PNG row keeps, besides `iCCP`, `sRGB`, `gAMA`, `cHRM` and the reduced `eXIf`:

- the APNG animation chunks `acTL`, `fcTL` and `fdAT`, so an animated PNG stays animated; the compressed frame data of `fdAT` is recompressed losslessly, as `IDAT` is. An animation that breaks the PNG specification keeps the frames before its first defect (below);
- the HDR colour chunks `cICP`, `mDCV` and `cLLI` of the PNG third edition, unchanged. `cICP` takes precedence over `iCCP` in a reader that knows it, so dropping it would change the colours.

Every other ancillary chunk is still dropped.

**A broken animation (D103).** The file is first checked as a still PNG is. `acTL`, `fcTL` and `fdAT` are ancillary chunks, which libpng, the reference of section 3 for a still PNG, skips, so a file that the still-image checks refuse is refused as corrupt whatever its animation holds. Among those refusals are a file cut short or without `IEND`, wherever the cut falls, and a default image that does not decode: an APNG cut short inside its animation is refused, as the same cut in a still PNG is, although Chromium and Firefox, read from their sources, show the frames before the cut. The maintainer kept that refusal on 2026-10-26 (D111, "Refuse it as corrupt, as built"; Context), so the rule of D103 below applies only to an animation broken inside an otherwise complete file. What follows applies to a file that those checks accept.

The animation is the one announced by the first `acTL` before the image data that is 8 bytes long and holds 1 to 2³¹ − 1 frames and at most 2³¹ − 1 plays. The `png` crate ignores every other intact `acTL`, and so does the optimiser; a PNG without such an `acTL` is a still image, and its `fcTL` and `fdAT` chunks are dropped. The frames are read in file order under the rules of the PNG specification (third edition, §11.3.6) as the `png` crate applies them, with three checks of the still image's data carried over to each frame: its CRC, its zlib Adler-32, and data that decodes to exactly its rows. Those three, and the `acTL` lost to its CRC and the second `fcTL` before the image data below, are stricter than the `png` crate, whose defaults skip an ancillary chunk with a wrong CRC, ignore the Adler-32, discard decoded data past a frame's rows, and take a second `fcTL` there that covers the canvas.

- **The frames.** When an `fcTL` comes before the image data, the default image is frame 0, which starts at that `fcTL` and ends with the image data. Every `fcTL` after the image data starts a frame, whose data is the `fdAT` chunks after it, and that frame ends at the next `fcTL` or at `IEND`.
- **The first defect** is the first of these, in file order:
  - an `fcTL` or `fdAT` whose sequence number is not the next one, counted from 0 over both chunk types, and an `fdAT` shorter than its 4-byte sequence number;
  - an `fcTL` that is not 26 bytes long, or whose frame is smaller than 1 × 1, reaches outside the canvas of `IHDR`, or has a dispose operation other than 0 to 2 or a blend operation other than 0 or 1;
  - a second `fcTL` before the image data, and a first one there whose frame does not cover the whole canvas;
  - an `fdAT` before the image data, an `fdAT` after it that no `fcTL` after the image data comes before, and an `fdAT` that another chunk parts from the `fdAT` chunks before it in its frame;
  - a frame after the image data that ends without an `fdAT`;
  - an `fcTL` or `fdAT` lost to its CRC, and an `acTL` before the image data lost to its CRC, which might have been the one that announces the animation (an `acTL` after the image data is ignored, intact or not);
  - a frame whose data does not start with one zlib stream, its Adler-32 matching, that decodes to exactly the frame's rows, each starting with a filter type from 0 to 4 (bytes after the end of that stream are neither read nor stored).
- **Where it lies.** A defective `fcTL` lies in the frame that it starts, or would start; both kinds of defective `fcTL` before the image data lie in frame 0. Any other defective chunk lies in the frame that has not ended when the chunk is read, or in no frame when no frame has started yet or every frame before it has ended, such as an `acTL` lost to its CRC before frame 0's `fcTL`, or an `fdAT` right after the image data. A frame after the image data that ends without an `fdAT`, or a frame whose data does not decode, is itself the defect and lies in that frame.
- **What is kept.** The frame in which the first defect lies is dropped, with every later frame, and the frames before it are kept; a defect that lies in no frame keeps every frame that ended before it. Of those, at most the number of frames that `acTL` announces is kept: a frame past that number is dropped whatever it holds, and is no defect. The stored `acTL` announces the number of frames kept, with its play count unchanged, and the kept chunks are numbered again from 0. When no frame is kept, `acTL`, `fcTL` and `fdAT` are all dropped and the still default image is stored, as for a PNG without animation.

The stored file stays no larger than the upload, because the kept frames are part of the upload's and `acTL` keeps its length. Visitors see the frames before the defect, which Chromium and Firefox both show (Context, D103). Frames that Chromium shows from the defect on are lost: those after a frame whose data alone is damaged, and those that the `png` crate keeps where the checks above are stricter. The totals of section 4 count the default image and the frames that the chunks alone keep: those that would be kept if no frame's data were damaged. They are counted before any image data is inflated, and no other frame's data is ever inflated.

**Implemented by:** image-optimise-png.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- Every stored re-encode meets ADR 0015 §7's own measure of "visually lossless", for JPEG and lossy WebP. A textured photograph loses only its saving, never detail.
- The corpus test no longer lists a file that misses the ADR's target; the camera-original case is in the corpus and is handled by the rule.
- What a browser decodes, backend3 accepts, as #211 already did for the headers; a file that Chromium turns by its EXIF Orientation is stored turned the same way.
- No crafted animation or JPEG can hold the one-at-a-time optimiser for longer than the limits allow, so one upload cannot block every editor's uploads past the request timeout.
- Animated PNGs and HDR PNGs display after upload as they did before, as GIF and WebP animations already do. A broken APNG still plays the frames before its first defect, which Chromium and Firefox both show, rather than freezing on its still image.
- GIF and WebP treat a file with no image the same way: it is refused as corrupt.

**Negative**

- Every re-encode that passes the size test costs one more decode and an SSIM pass: 1.3 to 1.8 CPU seconds at 50 megapixels on the measuring machine, more on a slower node.
- A progressive JPEG of more than 100 scans is refused, although libjpeg would decode it; no common encoder writes one.
- An APNG above the totals of section 4 is refused, as a WebP or GIF animation is; the costliest APNG measured within them took about 21 CPU seconds on the measuring machine, which the node timing of section 5 checks.
- A broken APNG loses every frame from its first defect on, also the frames after a frame whose data alone is damaged, which Chromium still shows, and the frames that Chromium shows where section 6 is stricter than the `png` crate (a CRC or Adler-32 that does not match, decoded data past a frame's rows, a second `fcTL` before the image data).
- An APNG that the still-image checks refuse, one cut short inside its animation among them (D111), is refused as corrupt, as a still PNG is, although Chromium and Firefox show the frames before the cut; its editor gets the corrupt-image message and uploads the file again.
- A GIF with no image is refused even where the `gif` crate decodes it, so section 3 has one exception to its rule.
- The 10 measured photographs below 0.98 now keep their stripped original, so their uploads save only what the metadata strip removes. The stored-to-stripped ratios of the measurement (0.199 and 0.590 at quality 85) were not measured again under the floor.
- 4:4:4 originals still lose half of their colour resolution in a kept re-encode, within the tested bound.
- PNGs above the threshold are a little larger than preset 2 would make them.
- More lenient parsing, the EXIF sub-IFD, the short EXIF replacement and the cut of a broken APNG are more code paths to test.

**Follow-up work**

- Merge order: this ADR lands with image-optimise-jpeg. image-optimise-webp-gif and image-optimise-png implement parts of it (sections 1 and 3 to 6), so they merge after it (ADR 0001 §6).
- image-optimise-webp-gif: the floor for still lossy WebP through `src/domain/image_optimise/ssim.rs`; the reference-decoder checks of section 3 and the refusal of a GIF with no image; the GIF totals; and its corpus test of a lossy WebP below the floor.
- image-optimise-png: the preset threshold as a validated setting; `acTL`, `fcTL` and `fdAT` kept with `fdAT` recompressed, and a broken animation cut at its first defect; `cICP`, `mDCV` and `cLLI` kept; the APNG totals.
- k8s-backend3-deploy: measure the PNG presets on the production node and set the threshold.
- media-upload-core: the Hungarian messages for the four refusals of section 4.

**What must be true in code and tests**

These bullets join ADR 0015's "Image tests":

- every re-encoded JPEG and lossy WebP in the corpus reaches a luma SSIM of at least 0.98 against the stripped original, measured with the same `mean_ssim` that the keep rule uses; and the corpus holds, for each of the two formats, a file whose re-encode is at least 5 % smaller but below the floor, and which keeps its stripped original;
- the SSIM module agrees with the definition of the measure (a direct 11 × 11 computation) on random images, and the keep rule is tested at 0.98 and just below it;
- a JPEG 4:4:4 original whose re-encode is kept stays within the chroma bound;
- each EXIF shape of section 3 has its own test, and no replacement is longer than the EXIF structure it replaces;
- a GIF with no image is refused as corrupt with and without its trailer, also when an extension comes before the trailer;
- each limit of section 4 is tested at the limit and one above it, and refused before any decode;
- an APNG keeps its animation, and `cICP`, `mDCV` and `cLLI` stay byte for byte;
- each kind of first defect of section 6 has its own test, which keeps exactly the frames before it, with `acTL` announcing their number, also for a defect that lies in no frame; frames past the announced number are dropped; an animation with no frame left keeps the still image; and an APNG cut short inside its animation is refused as corrupt;
- the PNG threshold selects preset 2 at and below it and the cheaper preset above it.
